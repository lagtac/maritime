## 1. Industry structure and parties

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Broker (shipbroker)** | Middleman who negotiates fixtures between owners and charterers. Paid a commission (usually 1.25% per broker). |
| **Charter chain** | Series of charters from the head owner down to the last sub-charterer. Each party is "owner" to the one below it. |
| **Charterer** | The party that hires the ship: a trader, miner, oil major, or another operator. |
| **Commercial manager** | Company that finds employment (charters) for a ship on the owner's behalf. |
| **Commodity trader** | Company that buys and sells physical commodities (oil, grain, metals, coal) and charters ships to move them, e.g. Vitol, Trafigura, Cargill. Often the biggest charterers; many also operate chartered-in fleets. |
| **Container / liner shipping** | Ships on fixed routes and published schedules carrying containers. Dominated by Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM etc. |
| **Disponent owner** | A party that is not the registered owner but controls the ship (e.g. under a time charter) and re-charters it out. |
| **DOC holder** | The company named on the ISM Document of Compliance; legally responsible for safe operation. Often the ship manager. |
| **Flag of convenience / open registry** | Registry open to foreign owners (e.g. Panama, Liberia, Marshall Islands, Malta). Most of the world fleet is flagged this way. |
| **Head owner** | The registered owner at the top of a charter chain. |
| **ISM Code** | IMO International Safety Management Code: the company must run a safety management system (SMS). Defines the DOC holder and Designated Person Ashore (DPA). |
| **Operator** | Company that runs the commercial side of voyages. Can own ships or charter them in. |
| **Physical supplier / bunker trader / bunker broker** | Physical supplier delivers fuel (own barges); trader buys from suppliers and resells with credit; broker only arranges the deal for a commission. |
| **Pool / pool manager** | Group of similar ships from several owners, marketed together by a pool manager. Earnings shared by points per ship. |
| **Registered owner** | The legal owner of the ship as shown in the ship registry. Often a single-ship company. |
| **Relet** | Chartering out a ship you have chartered in. |
| **Ship chandler** | Supplier of stores, provisions and spares to ships in port. |
| **Ship financier / lessor** | Bank or leasing house funding ships, often via mortgage or sale-and-leaseback (bareboat back to the seller). |
| **Ship manager (technical manager)** | Company that runs the ship day to day: crew, maintenance, certificates, safety. |
| **Sub-charterer** | A charterer who hires the ship from another charterer. |
| **Surveyor** | Independent inspector: on/off-hire condition, draft, bunker, hold/tank, cargo and damage surveys. |
| **Tramp shipping** | Ships without a fixed route, hired per voyage or period to wherever cargo is. Bulkers and tankers. Most Greek shipping. |

## 2. Vessel types and sizes

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Aframax** | Tanker, roughly 80,000–120,000 dwt. |
| **Barge** | Flat-bottomed vessel, often unpowered, used on rivers and in ports. |
| **Bulk carrier (bulker)** | Ship for dry bulk: grain, coal, iron ore, bauxite, fertiliser. |
| **Capesize** | Largest common bulker, ~150,000–210,000 dwt. Too big for the old Panama Canal, goes round the Cape. |
| **Chemical tanker** | Tanker with coated/stainless tanks for chemicals and clean products. |
| **Clean / dirty** | Tanker trades. Clean = refined light products (gasoline, diesel, jet, naphtha). Dirty = crude, fuel oil and other black oils. |
| **Container ship** | Carries containers measured in TEU. |
| **Crude tanker** | Tanker for unrefined crude oil (Aframax, Suezmax, VLCC). |
| **Dry bulk** | Unpackaged solid cargo loaded loose in holds. |
| **FPSO / FSO / FSRU** | Floating units: Production, Storage and Offloading / Storage and Offloading / Storage and Regasification (LNG import terminal on a ship). |
| **Handymax** | Older term for a geared bulker of ~40,000–50,000 dwt. Today mostly replaced by Supramax. |
| **Handysize** | Small bulker, ~15,000–40,000 dwt. |
| **Heavy lift / project cargo ship** | Ship with heavy cranes for oversized pieces (turbines, refinery modules). |
| **Kamsarmax** | Panamax-type bulker ~82,000 dwt, named after Port Kamsar (Guinea). |
| **LNG carrier** | Carries liquefied natural gas at about −162 °C. |
| **LPG carrier** | Carries liquefied petroleum gas (propane, butane). |
| **LR1 / LR2** | Long Range product tankers (~55–80k / ~80–160k dwt). |
| **MR** | Medium Range product tanker, ~40,000–55,000 dwt. |
| **Multipurpose (MPP) / general cargo ship** | Geared ship carrying breakbulk, project cargo, steel, containers or bulk as needed. |
| **Neopanamax / Post-Panamax** | Post-Panamax: too big for the old Panama locks. Neopanamax: largest size fitting the new locks (opened 2016). |
| **Newcastlemax** | Largest bulker able to enter Newcastle (Australia), ~200–210k dwt. |
| **Open-hatch bulker** | Bulker with box-shaped holds and full-width hatches, for forest products, paper, steel. |
| **Panamax** | Largest ship that fit the old Panama Canal locks. Bulkers ~65–80k dwt. |
| **PCTC / PCC** | Pure Car (and Truck) Carrier: Ro-Ro built for vehicles. |
| **Product tanker** | Carries refined oil products (gasoline, diesel, jet). "Clean" cargo. |
| **Reefer** | Refrigerated cargo ship or container. |
| **Ro-Ro** | Roll-on/roll-off: vehicles are driven on and off. |
| **Shuttle tanker** | Tanker that carries crude from offshore fields (e.g. North Sea, Brazil) to shore terminals, usually with dynamic positioning. |
| **Sister ship** | Ship of the same design built at the same yard. Used to benchmark performance and estimates. |
| **Suezmax** | Largest tanker that can transit Suez fully laden, ~120,000–200,000 dwt. |
| **Supramax / Ultramax** | Mid-size geared bulkers, ~50–60k / ~60–66k dwt. |
| **ULCC** | Ultra Large Crude Carrier, 320,000+ dwt. |
| **VLCC** | Very Large Crude Carrier, ~200,000–320,000 dwt. |
| **VLGC** | Very Large Gas Carrier (LPG), ~80,000+ m³. |
| **VLOC / Valemax** | Very Large Ore Carrier, ~250,000–400,000 dwt. Valemax (~400k dwt) was built for Vale's Brazil–China iron ore trade. |
| **Wet / liquid bulk** | Oil, chemicals and gas carried in tanks. |

## 3. Vessel particulars and measurements

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Air draft** | Height from waterline to the ship's highest point. Limits passage under bridges and some terminal loaders. |
| **Bale capacity** | Hold volume available for packaged cargo (less than grain capacity). |
| **Beam** | Width of the ship. |
| **Class / Classification society** | Organisation (DNV, LR, ABS, BV, ClassNK, RINA…) that sets technical standards and certifies a ship. |
| **Constants** | Weight on board not counted as cargo or fuel (stores, sludge, mud). Reduces intake. |
| **Displacement / lightweight (LWT)** | Displacement: total weight of the ship afloat. Lightweight: empty ship weight. Displacement = LWT + DWT. |
| **Draft (draught)** | Depth of the ship below the waterline. Limits which ports it can enter. |
| **DWCC** | Deadweight Cargo Capacity: DWT minus fuel, water, stores, constants. What's left for cargo. |
| **DWT / DWAT** | Deadweight (all told): total weight the ship can carry (cargo + fuel + water + stores + crew), in metric tonnes. |
| **Flag / Flag state** | Country where the ship is registered; its laws apply on board. |
| **Freeboard** | Vertical distance from the waterline to the main deck. |
| **Geared / gearless** | Ship has / lacks its own cranes for loading. |
| **Grain capacity** | Total hold volume, used for bulk cargo. |
| **GRT / GT** | Gross tonnage: a measure of the ship's internal volume, not weight. Used for fees and rule thresholds (e.g. 5,000 GT). |
| **Hatch covers** | Steel covers closing cargo holds. Must be weathertight; leaks cause wet-cargo claims. |
| **Heating coils** | Pipes in tanks to keep viscous cargo (fuel oil, some chemicals) pumpable. |
| **Holds / hatches / cranes** | Cargo space description for bulkers, e.g. "5 holds / 5 hatches, 4 x 30t cranes, grabs fitted". |
| **Ice class** | Hull and engine strengthening for ice navigation (e.g. Finnish-Swedish 1A, 1A Super). Required for some winter trades. |
| **IGS** | Inert Gas System: fills tanker cargo tanks with low-oxygen gas to prevent explosions. |
| **IMO number** | Unique 7-digit ship ID that never changes, even if name or flag changes. |
| **LOA** | Length overall. |
| **Load line** | Marks on the hull showing max draft by zone and season (Summer, Winter, Tropical, Fresh). Limits cargo intake. |
| **MMSI** | 9-digit radio/AIS identifier of a ship. |
| **NRT / NT** | Net tonnage: cargo-carrying volume. Used for canal and port dues. |
| **P&I entry / Class certificates** | Documents a charterer checks before fixing. |
| **Q88** | Standard tanker questionnaire describing the ship. Bulkers use a similar description form. |
| **SCNT / PCNT** | Suez Canal Net Tonnage / Panama Canal Net Tonnage, used to compute canal tolls. |
| **Segregations** | Number of separate cargo grades a tanker can carry without mixing (separate pumps and lines). |
| **Tank coating** | Lining of tanker cargo tanks (epoxy, zinc, stainless steel). Decides which chemicals/products can be carried. |
| **TEU / FEU** | Twenty-foot / Forty-foot Equivalent Unit: container size units. Container ship capacity is given in TEU. |
| **TPC** | Tonnes Per Centimetre immersion: extra cargo that sinks the ship by 1 cm. |
| **Year built (age)** | Ship age. Many charterers and terminals reject ships over 15–20 years; older ships attract overage insurance. |

## 4. Chartering and contracts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Addcomm** | Address commission: a discount paid back to the charterer, usually 2.5–3.75%. |
| **Back-to-back** | Chartering in and out on matching terms so risk passes through. |
| **Bareboat charter (BBC, demise)** | Charterer takes over the ship fully, including crew and running costs, for a long period. |
| **Brokerage** | Commission paid to brokers. |
| **Cancelling date** | Last day of the laycan. If the ship isn't ready by then, the charterer may cancel. |
| **Charter party (CP, C/P)** | The contract between owner and charterer. |
| **Charter-in / charter-out (TCI, TCO, VCI, VCO)** | Taking a ship or cargo in (you pay) vs giving it out (you earn). Time Charter In/Out, Voyage Charter In/Out. These are the main contract types in IMOS. |
| **COA** | Contract of Affreightment: agreement to carry a volume of cargo over a period, in several shipments, without naming one ship. |
| **Counter** | Reply to an offer with changed terms. |
| **Deadfreight** | Freight paid by the charterer for space booked but not filled (cargo short-loaded). |
| **Delivery / redelivery** | Start and end of a time charter: when and where the ship is handed over and returned. |
| **Delivery / redelivery range** | Area where a TC ship may be delivered or redelivered, e.g. "dely APS 1 SP Singapore–Japan range". |
| **Firm offer** | Binding offer valid for a stated time. |
| **Fixture / fixed** | A concluded charter deal. "We're fixed" = deal done. |
| **Freight** | Payment for carrying cargo under a voyage charter. Per tonne or lumpsum. |
| **Freight payment terms** | When freight is due, e.g. "95% within 3 banking days after signing B/Ls, balance after discharge" or BBB (before breaking bulk). |
| **Hire** | Daily rate paid under a time charter, usually paid 15 days in advance. |
| **Hire statement** | Running account under a TC: hire, off-hire, bunkers on delivery/redelivery, owners' expenses, commissions. Final hire statement settles at redelivery. |
| **Index-linked hire** | TC hire set as a % of a Baltic index average (e.g. 105% of BSI 10TC), reset periodically. |
| **Indication** | Non-binding price idea. |
| **Last cargoes** | Previous cargoes carried (often last 3). Checked for cleanliness and contamination risk, mainly on tankers. |
| **Last done** | Most recent comparable fixture. Used as a price reference in negotiation. |
| **Laycan** | Laydays/cancelling: the date window when the ship must be ready at load port. If late, charterer may cancel. |
| **Lumpsum** | Fixed freight amount regardless of quantity. |
| **Min / max period** | TC duration flexibility, e.g. "min 11 / max 13 months CHOPT". |
| **Off-hire** | Periods where the ship can't work due to owner's fault (breakdown, crew issue); hire stops. |
| **Open position** | Where and when a ship becomes free for the next job. |
| **Overage insurance (AP)** | Extra cargo insurance premium for ships over ~15 years. Usually charterer's account unless agreed otherwise. |
| **Owners' / charterers' account** | Who pays a given cost. |
| **P&C** | Private and Confidential: fixture terms not to be reported to the market. |
| **Period charter** | Time charter for months or years. |
| **Recap (fixture recap)** | Summary of all agreed terms, sent by the broker after fixing. The CP is drafted from it. |
| **Rider clauses** | Extra clauses added to a standard CP form. |
| **Scrubber benefit** | Split of fuel savings between owner and TC charterer on a scrubber-fitted ship (HSFO vs VLSFO spread). |
| **Spot** | Single-voyage, prompt business at current market rates. |
| **Subjects (subs)** | Conditions that must be met before a deal is binding (e.g. "sub board approval", "sub receivers"). "Subs lifted" = deal binding. |
| **TCT (trip time charter)** | Time charter for a single trip. |
| **Time charter (TC)** | Charterer hires ship for a period, directs it and pays fuel and port costs; owner pays crew and running costs. |
| **Tonnage list / cargo list** | Broker lists of ships open (by area and date) and of cargoes looking for ships. |
| **Voyage account / freight invoice** | Settlement under a VC: freight, deadfreight, demurrage/despatch, commissions, extras. |
| **Voyage charter (VC)** | Owner carries a cargo A→B for freight; owner pays fuel and port costs. |
| **Worldscale (WS)** | Tanker freight index. Rates quoted as a percentage of a flat rate per route (e.g. WS 85). |

## 5. Charter party forms and clauses

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AMWELSH / NORGRAIN** | Standard VC forms for coal (AMWELSH) and grain (NORGRAIN). |
| **ASBATANKVOY** | Standard tanker voyage CP form. |
| **BALTIME** | BIMCO time charter form (less common than NYPE). |
| **BIMCO Sanctions Clause** | Allocates rights and liabilities if a voyage would break sanctions. Now standard in most CPs. |
| **Both-to-blame collision clause** | Protects the owner from cargo claims recovered via the other ship in a collision where both are at fault. |
| **BPVOY / SHELLVOY** | Oil-major tanker voyage CP forms. |
| **Cesser clause** | Charterer's liability ends once cargo is loaded; owner relies on its lien for later sums. |
| **CII Operations Clause** | BIMCO clause on how charterer's orders affect the ship's CII rating. |
| **CONGENBILL** | BIMCO bill of lading form for use with GENCON charters. |
| **Employment and indemnity clause** | Master follows charterer's orders on the ship's employment; charterer indemnifies owner for the consequences. |
| **ETS Allowances Clause** | BIMCO clause deciding who provides EU ETS allowances (usually charterer under TC). |
| **FuelEU Maritime Clause** | BIMCO clause on who bears FuelEU penalties/benefits. |
| **GENCON** | BIMCO general dry-cargo voyage charter form (GENCON 94, GENCON 2022). |
| **Hull fouling clause** | Allocates risk and cleaning costs when a ship fouls after long idling on charterer's orders. |
| **Ice clause, war risks clause (CONWARTIME / VOYWAR)** | Clauses on trading in ice or war areas. |
| **Inter-Club Agreement (ICA)** | P&I clubs' formula for splitting cargo claims between owner and charterer under NYPE. |
| **Law and arbitration clause** | Chooses law and forum, e.g. English law, LMAA arbitration in London. |
| **Lien clause** | Owner's right to hold cargo and sub-freights for unpaid hire or freight. |
| **Logical amendments** | Recap wording meaning "adjust the form to fit what we agreed". |
| **NYPE** | New York Produce Exchange time charter form (NYPE 46, 93, 2015). Most common TC form. |
| **Off-hire clause** | Lists events when hire stops (breakdown, deficiency of crew, drydock etc.). NYPE clause 17 (NYPE 93). |
| **Paramount clause** | Incorporates the Hague / Hague-Visby Rules into the contract. |
| **Piracy clause** | Rights and costs when trading in piracy-risk areas (e.g. BIMCO Piracy Clause). |
| **Safe port warranty** | Charterer promises the nominated port is safe for the ship. Breach = charterer liable for damage. |
| **SHELLTIME / BPTIME** | Tanker time charter forms. |
| **SHIPMAN** | BIMCO standard ship management agreement (owner ↔ manager). |
| **Slow steaming clause** | Lets the charterer order reduced speed without breaching the speed warranty. |
| **SUPPLYTIME** | BIMCO time charter form for offshore support vessels. |
| **Withdrawal clause** | Owner may withdraw the ship if hire isn't paid on time, usually after an anti-technicality notice. |

## 6. Laytime and demurrage

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Arrived ship** | Point at which the ship may tender NOR. Depends on berth vs port charter. |
| **Berth charter / port charter** | Whether the voyage ends at the berth or on arrival in port. Decides who bears waiting time. |
| **Demurrage (DEM)** | Penalty paid by charterer to owner for each day beyond allowed laytime. |
| **Demurrage claim** | Owner's claim for demurrage, with laytime calc, SOF, NOR and CP. Must be filed before the time bar. |
| **Demurrage rate** | Daily amount paid when laytime is exceeded, e.g. USD 20,000 PDPR. |
| **Despatch (DES)** | Reward paid by owner to charterer for finishing cargo work early. Often half the demurrage rate (DHD). |
| **Detention** | Damages for delay not covered by demurrage. |
| **DHD / FD** | Despatch half demurrage / Free despatch. |
| **Excepted periods** | Events that don't count as laytime (e.g. rain, strikes, shifting), as listed in the CP. |
| **Half demurrage** | Some delays (e.g. weather, strikes on tanker forms) count at half the demurrage rate. |
| **Laytime** | Time allowed to load/discharge without penalty. |
| **Laytime calculation** | Statement computing time used vs allowed, giving demurrage or despatch. |
| **Load / discharge rate** | Laytime allowance as a rate, e.g. 10,000 MT PWWD SHEX. Allowed time = quantity / rate. |
| **NOR** | Notice of Readiness: master's notice that the ship has arrived and is ready. Starts laytime after a notice period. |
| **Notice time** | Hours after NOR tender before laytime starts (same idea as turn time), e.g. 6 hours. |
| **Once on demurrage, always on demurrage** | Once demurrage starts, exceptions (like weather) no longer stop it. |
| **Reachable on arrival (ROA)** | Charterer must provide a berth the ship can reach on arrival; waiting is charterer's risk. |
| **Reversible laytime** | Load and discharge time pooled into one allowance. |
| **Shifting** | Moving between anchorage and berth or between berths. CP says whether shifting time counts. |
| **SHINC / SHEX** | Sundays and Holidays Included / Excluded in laytime. |
| **SOF** | Statement of Facts: timeline of events in port, signed by agent/master. Basis of laytime calc. |
| **Time bar** | Deadline to submit a claim (e.g. demurrage within 90 days) or it's lost. |
| **Turn time** | Free hours after NOR before laytime starts. |
| **WIBON / WIPON / WIFPON / WCCON** | Whether in berth / port / free pratique / customs cleared or not: NOR can be tendered even if these aren't done yet. |
| **WWD** | Weather Working Day: a day when weather allows cargo work. |

## 7. Voyage estimation and commercial metrics

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ballast** | Sailing empty (no cargo), usually to the load port. Also: seawater carried for stability. |
| **Ballast bonus** | Extra payment to cover ballast leg to a remote delivery point. |
| **Baltic route codes** | Standard routes assessed by the Baltic Exchange, e.g. C5 (W Australia–Qingdao ore, Capesize), TD3C (MEG–China crude, VLCC), TC2 (Rotterdam–New York clean, MR). |
| **Break-even** | Rate that covers all costs. |
| **Commission (total)** | Addcomm + brokerage, deducted from freight or hire in the estimate. |
| **Daily capital cost** | Financing/depreciation cost per day. Used with OPEX for owner break-even. |
| **Delivery / ballast leg** | Sailing from the last discharge (or TC delivery) to the next load port. Its days and fuel are part of the estimate. |
| **Eco speed** | Slower speed to save fuel. |
| **Estimate (voyage estimate)** | Pre-fixture calculation of a voyage's profit and TCE. |
| **Estimate vs actual (variance)** | Comparison of the pre-fixture estimate with the final voyage result. |
| **FFA** | Forward Freight Agreement: financial derivative to hedge freight rates. |
| **Freight tax** | Tax on freight earned in certain countries. A voyage cost in the estimate. |
| **Intake** | How much cargo actually fits, after draft, load line and fuel limits. |
| **Laden** | Sailing loaded with cargo. |
| **OPEX** | Operating expenses: crew, maintenance, insurance, stores. Owner's cost. |
| **P&L** | Profit and loss of a voyage. |
| **Sea margin / weather factor** | % added to sea time to allow for weather and currents. |
| **Stowage factor (SF)** | Volume one tonne of cargo occupies (m³/t or ft³/lt). Decides if a ship "cubes out" before it "weighs out". |
| **TCE** | Time Charter Equivalent: (freight − voyage costs) / voyage days. Net $/day, used to compare voyages. |
| **Timecharter average (TCA)** | Weighted average of Baltic TC routes for a ship size, e.g. BCI 5TC, BSI 10TC. Basis for index-linked hire and FFAs. |
| **Utilisation** | Share of time a ship is laden and earning vs ballast or idle. Also ballast/laden ratio. |
| **Voyage costs** | Bunkers, port costs, canal dues, commissions, emissions costs. |
| **Voyage days** | Total days from start to end of voyage, including sea and port time. |

## 8. Operations and voyage execution

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **AIS** | Automatic Identification System: transponder broadcasting ship position, speed, identity. Source for tracking platforms. |
| **Arrival / departure report** | Messages sent by ship on arrival/departure with times and fuel figures. |
| **Ballast water management (BWM)** | IMO convention: ships must treat ballast water to stop spreading marine organisms. Treatment systems (BWTS) required. |
| **Beaufort scale** | Wind force scale 0–12. Used in "good weather" definitions (e.g. up to BF 4) and noon reports. |
| **Berthing prospects / line-up** | Agent's report of ships waiting and expected berthing order. Drives ETAs, speed orders and demurrage expectations. |
| **Bunker survey** | Independent measurement of fuel on board, e.g. at delivery/redelivery. |
| **COSP / EOSP** | Commencement / End Of Sea Passage: timestamps that split sea time from port time. Key for speed, consumption and emissions calculations. |
| **CP speed and consumption** | Speed and fuel use warranted in a time charter. Performance claims arise if the ship underperforms. |
| **Crew change** | Replacing crew in port. Often causes deviation or delay. |
| **Deviation** | Leaving the agreed route. May breach the CP and void insurance unless allowed (e.g. to save life, for bunkers if permitted). |
| **Drydock / special survey** | Periodic dry-docking for hull work and class renewal (roughly every 2.5–5 years). Ship is off-hire. |
| **ETA / ETB / ETD / ETC** | Estimated Time of Arrival / Berthing / Departure / Completion. |
| **ETA notices** | Notices the master must send before arrival (e.g. 7/5/3/2/1 days), as required by the CP. |
| **Good weather** | Conditions (e.g. up to Beaufort 4) used to judge speed/consumption performance. |
| **Heavy weather damage** | Damage to ship or cargo from bad weather. Needs noting in logs and often a note of protest. |
| **Hold cleaning / hold inspection** | Bulker holds must pass a surveyor's inspection (e.g. "grain clean") before loading. Failure delays laytime. |
| **Hull fouling** | Marine growth on the hull that slows the ship and raises consumption. |
| **Lay-up** | Taking a ship out of service for a period (hot or cold lay-up) when rates are too low. |
| **Lightering (STS)** | Transferring cargo between ships at sea, e.g. to reduce a big tanker's draft. |
| **Master** | The captain. |
| **Mooring / unmooring** | Securing the ship to the berth with lines, with linesmen and tugs. |
| **Noon report** | Daily report from the ship: position, speed, distance, weather, fuel consumed, ROB. Core data for performance and emissions. |
| **Note of protest** | Master's formal statement about an event (heavy weather, delay, damage), used to protect owners in later claims. |
| **On-hire / off-hire survey** | Joint survey of condition and bunkers at TC delivery and redelivery. |
| **Performance claim** | Charterer's claim when ship burned more fuel or was slower than warranted. |
| **Performance monitoring** | Checking noon report data against CP warranties, often by a third party using weather data. |
| **Port call** | One visit of a ship to a port: arrival, anchorage, berthing, cargo work, bunkering, departure. The basic unit for agents, PDA/FDA, laytime and emissions reporting. |
| **Port congestion / waiting time** | Time spent waiting for a berth. Who pays depends on berth/port charter and NOR terms. |
| **Port rotation / itinerary** | Order of ports on a voyage. |
| **ROB** | Remaining On Board: fuel (or water) quantity left. |
| **Slow steaming** | Deliberate low speed to cut fuel use and emissions. |
| **Speed orders / instructed speed** | Speed the charterer or operator tells the master to sail. Basis for performance checks. |
| **Tank cleaning** | Washing tanker tanks between cargoes. Takes time and costs; depends on last and next cargo. |
| **UKC** | Under Keel Clearance: water depth below the keel. Ports set minimums that limit draft. |
| **Vetting (SIRE / RightShip)** | Charterer's ship approval process. SIRE (OCIMF) inspections for tankers; RightShip ratings mainly for dry bulk. |
| **Voyage instructions** | Operator's orders to the master for a voyage. |
| **Weather routing** | Service that recommends routes to avoid bad weather. |

## 9. Bunkers (fuel)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BDN** | Bunker Delivery Note: receipt stating quantity and quality of fuel supplied. |
| **Biofuel / B24, B30** | Fuel blended with bio-components (e.g. 24% bio). Helps FuelEU and ETS. |
| **Boil-off gas (BOG) / heel** | LNG that evaporates during the voyage / LNG kept on board after discharge to keep tanks cold. |
| **Bunker clause** | CP clause on quantities and prices of fuel at delivery/redelivery. |
| **Bunker delivery methods** | Barge, ex-pipe (at berth from shore) or truck. |
| **Bunker pricing method** | How consumed fuel is valued in the P&L: FIFO, LIFO or average cost. Configurable in IMOS. |

## 13. Emissions and regulation

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Administering authority** | EU member state responsible for a shipping company under EU ETS/MRV. |

| **MARPOL** | IMO convention on ship pollution. Annex VI covers air emissions. |

| **Surrender** | Handing allowances over to the regulator to cover a year's emissions. |
| **Tank-to-wake / well-to-wake** | Emissions from burning fuel only / from production through combustion. |

| **UK ETS** | UK emissions trading scheme, extended to shipping from July 2026. |
| **Verifier** | Accredited body checking emissions reports (e.g. DNV, LR, BV, Verifavia). |

## 16. Commodity trading, risk and finance

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Accruals / month-end close** | Booking earned-but-uninvoiced revenue and incurred-but-unbilled costs each month. IMOS generates voyage accruals from P&L snapshots. |
| **Book / portfolio** | Grouping of trades for P&L and risk (by desk, trader, commodity or region). |
| **Bunker swap** | Derivative fixing a future fuel price. Hedges bunker cost in estimates and TC contracts. |
| **Cleared vs OTC** | Cleared trades go through a clearing house (margin, low credit risk); OTC trades are bilateral. |
| **Credit risk / credit limit** | Risk a counterparty doesn't pay; limits cap exposure per counterparty. |
| **Deal (trade) capture** | Recording a trade's terms (price, quantity, dates, delivery) in the CTRM. |
| **EUA futures** | Exchange-traded futures used to buy or hedge EU ETS allowances. |
| **Exposure / position** | Net amount at risk to a price: e.g. open freight days, unhedged bunkers, EUAs owed, EUR costs vs USD revenue. |
| **Forward curve** | Market prices for future periods (freight, bunkers, EUAs). Used for mark-to-market. |
| **Front / middle / back office** | Trading and chartering / risk and P&L control / settlement, invoicing and accounting. |
| **FX risk** | Most revenue is in USD, but some costs (EU ETS, EU ports, crew) are in EUR or other currencies. |
| **Hedge** | Trade that offsets a price risk, e.g. selling FFAs against chartered-in tonnage, bunker swaps against fuel purchases. |
| **Incoterms and who charters** | FOB: buyer charters the ship. CFR/CIF: seller charters. Decides who carries freight and demurrage risk. |
| **Mark-to-market (MTM)** | Valuing open positions at current market prices. Gives unrealised P&L. |
| **Nomination / scheduling** | Notice of ship, quantity and dates for a cargo under a sale contract; CTRM scheduling tracks it through to delivery. |
| **Physical vs paper** | Physical: actual cargo or ship. Paper: derivatives (FFAs, swaps, futures) settled in cash. |
| **Realised vs unrealised P&L** | Locked-in result of closed trades vs paper result of open positions. |
| **Settlement / netting** | Paying and receiving amounts due; netting offsets opposite amounts with the same counterparty. |
| **Trading demurrage** | Traders face demurrage on both sides: from the shipowner and to/from buyers/sellers under sale contracts. Mismatches are a key cost. |
| **VaR** | Value at Risk: estimated maximum loss on a portfolio over a period at a confidence level. |
