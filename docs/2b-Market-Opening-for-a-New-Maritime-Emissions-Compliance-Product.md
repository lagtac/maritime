# Is There a Market Opening for a New Maritime Emissions-Compliance Product? A 2026 Assessment for a Piraeus Builder

## TL;DR

- **The owner-side "generate the ETS/FuelEU invoice and send it to the charterer" category is effectively saturated** — OceanScore alone had surpassed 2,300 contracted vessels by July 2025 and reported working with "more than 100 customers" and "over 2,500 vessels globally" at ~USD 5m ARR by February 2026, and DNV, Veson, ABS Wavesight, BetterSea, Hecla, EmissionLink and a dozen others all cover it. A generic "me-too" invoicing ledger for owners is not a viable wedge in September 2026.
- **A credible opening remains, but it is narrow and specific**: (1) charterer-side reconciliation/audit of the ETS-FuelEU invoices charterers *receive* (explicitly acknowledged as unsolved by Coach Solutions and ZeroNorth); (2) a genuinely low-friction, low-cost tier for 3–10-ship Greek tramp owners who still run spreadsheets; (3) a disputes/evidence-pack layer for legacy fixtures and off-hire allocation, where arbitrations are already surfacing; and (4) IMO Net-Zero Framework readiness once the framework's fate resolves at the December 2026 reconvened session.
- **Regulatory churn is a tailwind, not a reset**: the Commission's 17 July 2026 EU ETS/MRV/FuelEU revision, UK ETS maritime (live since 1 July 2026), and the still-unadopted IMO NZF all change what a ledger must compute — but incumbents are already announcing support, so timing advantage for a newcomer is measured in months, not years.

## Key Findings

1. **The core owner→charterer invoicing function is a crowded, well-capitalised field.** At minimum OceanScore, DNV Emissions Connect, Veson (IMOS), ABS Wavesight, BetterSea, Hecla, EmissionLink, ZeroNorth, Siglar, StormGeo, NAVTOR, Lloyd's Register (VERS) and the Greek performance vendors (Metis, DeepSea, Danaos, Prisma/LAROS via Danaos) all touch ETS/FuelEU. The ones that explicitly do owner–charterer *settlement/invoicing* are OceanScore, DNV Emissions Connect, Veson IMOS, Lloyd's Register VERS, ZeroNorth, Hecla and EmissionLink. The rest are primarily reporting/verification/estimation.

2. **Greek adoption is real and accelerating, dominated by outsourced/managed services and a few software platforms.** OceanScore has opened a Greece office and counts Greek-linked names (Eurotankers, Almi Marine, Eurobulk, Cetus Maritime); ABS Wavesight signed Navios and Navilands; Latsco presented FuelEU pooling strategy publicly. But adoption is concentrated among larger, more sophisticated owners; the long tail of 3–40-ship tramp owners is materially less penetrated and still spreadsheet-heavy.

3. **Unsolved pain is concentrated in settlement, not calculation.** The calculation engines are commoditising; the disputes are contractual and evidentiary — legacy fixtures with no ETS/FuelEU clause, off-hire allowance adjustment, charterer non-transfer of EUAs, pooling interactions, and data-quality/noon-report disputes. London and Singapore arbitrations involving EUA transfer and FuelEU penalties have already appeared (e.g., London Arbitration 4/26). A July 2026 academic study concluded the EU ETS statutory "reimbursement right" is largely unenforceable in English-law/arbitration-governed charters.

4. **Small owners (3–10 ships) are structurally under-served on price and friction.** No major platform publishes per-vessel pricing; all are quote-based, and several (Hecla, EmissionLink) are outsourced managed services priced for scale. Spreadsheets remain the default for smaller operators — explicitly acknowledged by ZeroNorth and ZERO44 — and paid Excel models are actively sold to them.

5. **The charterer side is the clearest white space.** No vendor was found marketing a standalone "audit the ETS/FuelEU invoice you received" product for time/voyage charterers. The nearest offerings are LR VERS (bilateral per-voyage validation), DNV Emissions Connect (shared verified data), Veson's 2026 "Agreed Rebill" + DNV-verified data, and ZeroNorth Scope 3 — none of which is a dedicated charterer reconciliation tool.

**Reading the map.** The vendors that genuinely close the owner→charterer settlement loop — OceanScore, DNV Emissions Connect, Veson IMOS, LR VERS, Hecla, EmissionLink — are either well-funded specialists, class societies, or the dominant commercial-management ERP (Veson). The Greek-based players (Signal Ocean, Metis, DeepSea, Danaos, Prisma) are strong on data and performance but comparatively thin on the *commercial settlement* layer, with Danaos closest via its ERP and Metis offering EU ETS/FuelEU modules. Note the enormous overlap between "compliance software" and "managed service": Hecla (1,000+ vessels) and EmissionLink (600+ vessels pooled) are essentially outsourced compliance desks with a portal, not self-serve SaaS.

*Source-quality flag:* Most functional detail above is drawn from vendor marketing pages and press releases and should be treated as claims, not independently verified capability. Customer counts (OceanScore's 2,300–2,500 vessels; Hecla's 1,000+; BetterSea's 5,000 "onboarding") are self-reported.

### 2. Adoption evidence in Greece

- **OceanScore** opened a Greece office and reports Greek and Greek-linked customers including **Eurotankers** (tanker operator), **Almi Marine** (dry bulk), **Eurobulk** (tramp), and **Cetus Maritime** (dry bulk),\[1\] alongside third-party managers Anglo-Eastern and V.Ships. It surpassed **2,300 contracted vessels** in its 30 July 2025 press release, and by its 2 February 2026 announcement reported working with **"more than 100 customers" supporting "over 2,500 vessels globally"** at **USD 5 million in annual recurring revenue**.
- **ABS Wavesight** signed **Navios Maritime Partners** (July 2025)\[2\] and **Navilands Group** (Oct 2025, CEO Panagiotis Foinikis) for FuelEU/EU ETS compliance across bulkers and containerships.\[3\]
- **Latsco Marine Management** presented FuelEU pooling strategy at the 2025 GREEN4SEA Athens Forum (Dimitris Mytilinis, Senior Performance Engineer), signalling active engagement among larger Greek managers.
- **Union of Greek Shipowners** 2025–2026 Annual Report reports the Greek-owned fleet grew to **458m dwt = 19.1% of the global fleet, with 5,798 ships** — including **2,766 bulk carriers (22% of the global dry bulk fleet), 1,064 tankers (26% of the world tanker fleet), and 172 LNG carriers (23%)**. The UGS takes a hostile policy line, arguing EU ETS and FuelEU "must be withdrawn"\[4\] and framing them as measures that "create distortions without delivering meaningful environmental results" (President Travlos) — a political stance that coexists with practical compliance investment. The report notes energy-saving technology adoption rose from **"one in three" ships in early 2024 to "more than 50% of the fleet"** today.
- **OceanScore's own analysis** (co-MD Ralf Garrn, via Seatrade Maritime) put full-implementation EU ETS costs for the Greek fleet at **€837m in 2026** (potentially **over €1bn** with a moderate EUA price rise) and **€400,000 per vessel** at full implementation — based on Greek owners surrendering ~11.96m EUAs on 2022 voyage data (€335m at 40% in 2024, €586m at 70% in 2025). This quantification itself functions as marketing.
- Greek performance vendors (**Metis, DeepSea/Nabtesco, Danaos, Prisma/LAROS**) are widely deployed for data/performance but are not the primary vehicle for owner–charterer settlement.

*Judgment:* Larger Greek owners and third-party managers are adopting named platforms; the 3–40-ship tramp segment is demonstrably less penetrated and remains a mix of spreadsheets, verifier-led managed services, and ERP modules. Independent survey data quantifying the spreadsheet share among Greek mid-size owners was not located in this research and should be validated directly (see Recommendations).

### 3. Evidence of unsolved pain

- **Legacy fixtures & silence in contracts.** Coach Solutions (2026): "ETS has turned every EU voyage into a financial reconciliation event. Charterers are now settling allowance costs monthly, sometimes on fuel data they cannot fully verify… The fixtures being signed in 2026 read differently from the ones now being unwound in dispute."\[5\] Older charters that pre-date BIMCO ETS/FuelEU clauses are the fault line.
- **Charterer non-transfer of EUAs.** EmissionLink (Sept 2026, MD Philippos Ioulianou): owners may delay buying allowances because the charterer is contractually liable, while charterers delay because the contract permits it — "the company responsible for compliance remains accountable to the regulator, so that mismatch creates avoidable market risk." The phase-in to 100% for 2026 emissions sharpens this.
- **Reimbursement right largely unenforceable.** A July 2026 study (reported via Cyprus Shipping News) found the EU ETS statutory reimbursement mechanism has "little practical value for the vast majority of the market" because English-law charters and London/Singapore arbitration sit outside EU court jurisdiction and English law provides no reimbursement mechanism.\[6\]
- **Arbitrations already surfacing.** In **London Arbitration 4/26**, a vessel chartered on an amended NYPE 1946 form for a single trip (Nouadhibou→Taranto) with the BIMCO ETS Allowances Clause 2022 and BIMCO FuelEU Maritime Clause 2024 incorporated, owners claimed **US$13,190.34 in outstanding hire, a FuelEU Maritime penalty of €12,710.10, and the transfer of 475.04 EUAs**; the charterers counterclaimed US$16,779.22 for hull fouling. Under the LMAA Small Claims Procedure 2021, the sole arbitrator found the owners' claim "succeeded in its entirety." Small sums individually, but indicative of disputes now reaching formal award.
- **Off-hire and pooling allocation.** Watson Farley & Williams (2026): compensation between parties on vessel sale/transfer must account for "periods of off-hire, owners acting contrary to charterer instructions, underperformance… the 10% multiplier uplift on the penalty that may have been inherited from a previous charterer, or pooling decisions that may have impacted the compliance balance… Disputes are inevitable, because the speedy development and implementation of FuelEU has left many gaps."
- **Data quality as a financial risk.** DNV: annual inaccuracies tolerable under EU MRV now produce "significant financial discrepancies" because settlement is per-voyage; "Data integrity has become… a vital financial strategy."\[7\] Hafnia (via Veson/DNV) reported spending "significant time toggling between systems to reconcile emissions data."
- **Regulatory plumbing still incomplete.** WFW noted the FuelEU Database was not fully operational and several implementing acts (onshore power, zero-emission tech criteria, transhipment ports) were still pending as the first deadline passed.\[8\]

*Judgment:* This is the strongest evidence in the whole report. The pain has migrated *from calculation to settlement and evidence*. The disputes are about trust in the number, allocation of off-hire/pool effects, and enforceability across the charter chain — not about whether someone can compute EUAs.

### 4. Under-served segments

**(a) Small owners (3–10 ships).** No major platform publishes pricing; all are quote-based. ZERO44 (Berlin) confirms a per-vessel-per-year SaaS model but publishes no figure ("Software as a service fee per vessel per year applies, please contact zero44 for an individual price quote"). DNV and BetterSea offer free trials/free tiers; Siglar offers free EU ETS analysis and serves "one or one thousand ships." Managed services (Hecla, EmissionLink) are priced for scale. ZeroNorth and ZERO44 explicitly acknowledge that smaller teams "still run on fragmented systems and spreadsheets," and paid Excel models (Sustainable Ships, Ortech Marine) are marketed straight at them. **This is a real gap: friction and price, not absence of a calculator.**

**(b) Charterer side.** The clearest white space. No standalone "audit the invoice you received" product was found. LR VERS provides bilateral per-voyage validation (claiming 70 companies, 8,000+ reports, €100m+ in payments) with "LR Advisory's regulatory experts that review every transaction report before it is delivered"; DNV Emissions Connect provides a shared single source of truth; Veson added DNV-verified data + "Agreed Rebill" (isolate, agree, rebill emissions costs with a traceable audit trail) in H1 2026; ZeroNorth Scope 3 does charterer emissions reporting. But a lightweight tool that ingests an owner's ETS/FuelEU statement and independently checks the EUA count, the 50/100% scope split, off-hire deductions and pool treatment — from the charterer's perspective — does not visibly exist.

**(c) Technical managers running many third-party owners.** Partly served — OceanScore (V.Ships, Anglo-Eastern), Hecla and EmissionLink all target DOC-holding managers. The unsolved piece is multi-principal segregation and per-owner billing/audit trails at scale, which is more a depth-of-feature gap than a green field.

**(d) Data-quality/noon-report normalisation as a standalone product.** Emerging but not owned. DNV's automated feedback loop,\[7\] StormGeo's quarterly data-quality reviews,\[9\] and NAVTOR's dual-layer validation all bundle this into larger suites. A neutral, vendor-agnostic "noon-report normalisation + evidence pack" layer that feeds *any* downstream compliance/settlement engine is a plausible niche, especially given the disputes over data trust.

### 5. Regulatory churn that could reopen the market

- **EU ETS/MRV/FuelEU revision (tabled 17 July 2026).** COM(2026) 616 and accompanying regulations. Key maritime changes: merged/"report once" reporting across ETS, MRV and FuelEU — the Commission estimates this "report once" rule will "slice administrative compliance costs for shipping companies by 10% to 20%"; alignment of the "responsible entity" by replacing FuelEU's standalone company definition with the ETS "shipping company" concept;\[10\] extension of MRV to oil/chemical/gas/LNG carriers, Ro-Pax and passenger ships below 5,000 GT (but ≥400 GT) from 2029; offshore operations brought into ETS;\[10\] a new allowance reserve (proposed Art. 3gaa) for sustainable maritime fuels and zero-emission propulsion; an "ETS-as-a-service" option for third countries; and an IMO deduction mechanism to avoid double payment. Adoption targeted for **end of Q1 2027** under the "One Europe, One Market" process. The Commission itself acknowledges that contracts "may provide for the transfer of allocated allowances," meaning ETS clauses that only cover *surrendered* allowances "may become incomplete" — a direct signal that settlement logic will need to change.
- **UK ETS maritime — live since 1 July 2026.** Per DNV and Lloyd's Register class news 06/2026: covers cargo and passenger ships ≥5,000 GT on domestic UK voyages and at UK berths (100% domestic; 50% GB↔NI). First reporting period is a six-month stub (1 Jul–31 Dec 2026); Emissions Monitoring Plan due within 42 days of first activity; verified report due 31 March 2027; first surrender is a one-off "double surrender" for 2026+2027 due **30 April 2028**; managed via the METS system (no ship-level Document of Compliance). Extension to offshore ships from 1 Jan 2027;\[11\] proposed extension to international voyages from 2028 subject to EU negotiations.\[12\] UK–EU ETS linkage announced as intent but not yet in place.\[13\] This adds a *parallel* ledger (different scope, GWP values CH₄=28/N₂O=265, tank-to-wake,\[14\] earlier surrender date) that every UK-touching ship must now compute alongside EU ETS.
- **IMO Net-Zero Framework — not yet adopted; decision pushed to December 2026.** The 17 October 2025 extraordinary MEPC session adjourned for a year on a Saudi Arabia motion that passed **57 in favour, 49 against, 21 abstaining** (Greece and Cyprus abstained; most EU states and the UK voted in favour). US Secretaries Rubio, Wright and Duffy had issued a 10 October 2025 joint statement "unequivocally reject[ing]" the framework. MEPC 84 (27 Apr–1 May 2026) kept the NZF alive "unchanged," established intersessional working groups,\[15\] and advanced (but did not finalise) LCA guidelines, the GFI registry and reward mechanisms;\[16\] the Net-Zero Fund remains among the least-developed elements.\[17\] The resumed decision is scheduled around MEPC 85 (30 Nov–3 Dec 2026) and a one-day reconvened extraordinary session (4 Dec 2026).\[15\] Earliest MARPOL Annex VI entry into force ~1 March 2028. US and oil-producing-state opposition makes the outcome genuinely uncertain.\[15\]
- **CII review at IMO.** MEPC 84 adopted updated CII Guidelines (G1) and SEEMP amendments\[18\] but did *not* agree new CII metrics (an adverse-weather metric and a cruise cgHRS metric were discussed and deferred).\[19\] Phase 2 of the short-term-measure review runs to ~Spring 2028 (MEPC 87), explicitly to align the CII framework with the NZF.\[20\]

*Judgment:* Every one of these changes what a compliance ledger must compute — most importantly the EU "report once" merge and responsible-entity alignment, the new UK parallel ledger, and (contingently) a third global NZF/GFI ledger from 2028. Incumbents (OceanScore, DNV, BetterSea, Siglar via its Scheme Navigator) are already publishing on all of them, so the newcomer's regulatory-timing advantage is modest and perishable.

### 6. Verdict

**Is the owner-side ETS/FuelEU invoicing category saturated? Yes.** For a solo full-stack developer to build another "ingest noon reports → compute EUAs/FuelEU → invoice the charterer → send to owner" product for owners is to enter head-on against OceanScore (2,500+ vessels, ~USD 5m ARR), DNV, Veson, ABS, and a field of managed-service desks (Hecla, EmissionLink) with class-society and P&I distribution. Do not do this.

**Where the opening actually is (ranked by credibility):**

1. **Charterer-side reconciliation / invoice-audit (strongest).** *For:* explicitly acknowledged unsolved pain (Coach, ZeroNorth); no standalone product found; charterers settle monthly on data "they cannot fully verify"; the buyer (charterer/operator) has a direct, quantifiable financial motive to catch over-billing. *Against:* incumbents (LR VERS, DNV, Veson) are circling the same trust problem from the data-provenance side; a pure audit tool needs access to the owner's underlying data to be authoritative.
2. **Small-owner low-friction tier (strong, but commoditising).** *For:* 3–10-ship Greek tramp owners are price- and friction-excluded; spreadsheets are still the default; Piraeus proximity and Greek-language support are a genuine moat against Hamburg/Oslo/Boston vendors. *Against:* it's a race to the bottom on price; DNV/BetterSea free tiers and Excel models already occupy the floor; low ARPU means you need volume and cheap acquisition.
3. **Disputes/evidence-pack layer (strong, differentiated).** *For:* arbitrations are appearing; off-hire, pool-effect and legacy-fixture allocation are unsolved; a product that assembles a defensible, verifier-linked evidence pack per disputed voyage is novel and high-value. *Against:* small deal count today; sales cycle runs through lawyers and claims teams, not IT budgets.
4. **Data-quality/noon-report normalisation as a neutral layer (plausible niche).** *For:* data trust is the root of most disputes; a vendor-agnostic normaliser could feed any downstream engine. *Against:* incumbents bundle it; hard to charge for a "plumbing" layer.
5. **IMO NZF/GFI readiness (option value, not yet).** *For:* if adopted in Dec 2026, a third global ledger arrives ~2028 and everyone re-tools. *Against:* adoption is genuinely uncertain; building ahead of a framework that may be gutted is speculative.

**The sharpest single wedge:** a **charterer-side reconciliation tool with a disputes/evidence-pack module**, sold first to Piraeus-based operators and charterers who *receive* ETS/FuelEU invoices, with a cheap self-serve tier that also catches small tramp owners. This attacks the one function no incumbent owns, uses the builder's Athens location as distribution, and rides the settlement-not-calculation shift in the pain evidence.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Validate before building (next 4–6 weeks).** Run the validation conversations below. **Kill criterion:** if 3+ charterers/operators say they already trust owners' invoices or that Veson/DNV/LR VERS fully solves their reconciliation, stop. **Proceed criterion:** if 3+ describe manual spot-checking in Excel, past over-billing they caught or suspect, or disputes they couldn't evidence.

**Stage 1 — If validated, build the thin wedge (months 2–5).** A single-purpose tool: upload/parse an owner's ETS/FuelEU statement + the fixture terms → independently recompute EUAs, the 50/100% scope split, off-hire deductions and pool treatment → flag discrepancies → output a one-page evidence pack. Charge per-vessel-per-year at a price a 5-ship owner will pay without procurement (benchmark against ZERO44's per-vessel SaaS and DNV/BetterSea free tiers). **Threshold to expand:** 10 paying customers or 100 vessels within two quarters.

**Stage 2 — Add the small-owner compliance tier and Greek-language onboarding (months 4–9).** Only after the charterer wedge shows pull. **Threshold to add NZF/GFI:** IMO adoption at the Dec 2026 reconvened session; until then, build EU "report once" and UK ETS parallel-ledger support, which are certain.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** (i) if OceanScore or Veson ship a dedicated charterer-audit module, pivot to the disputes/evidence-pack niche where class societies are slower; (ii) if the Dec 2026 NZF vote fails, deprioritise global-ledger work indefinitely; (iii) if EU Q1-2027 adoption confirms "report once," ensure your ledger consumes the merged report natively.

**The 5–8 highest-value Piraeus validation conversations:**
1. **A mid-size tramp owner (5–15 ships)** — "Show me exactly how you compute and invoice EUAs/FuelEU today. What's in Excel? What broke at the April 2026 FuelEU deadline?" *Confirms if:* spreadsheets + pain. *Kills if:* already on OceanScore/DNV and happy.
2. **A time/voyage charterer or operator** — "When you receive an owner's ETS/FuelEU invoice, how do you check it? Have you ever caught an error?" *Confirms if:* manual/no check + suspected errors. *Kills if:* they trust it or delegate to Veson/DNV.
3. **A chartering/operations manager at a Greek house that both owns and charters in** — tests the two-sided reconciliation value directly.
4. **A Piraeus P&I correspondent or marine lawyer (HFW/Ince/Reed Smith Piraeus)** — "How many ETS/FuelEU settlement disputes are you seeing? What evidence is missing?" *Confirms if:* rising disputes, poor evidence. *Kills if:* trivial volume.
5. **A verifier office in Piraeus (Verifavia/DNV/LR/BV/RINA/ABS)** — "How many mid-size owners still hand you spreadsheets? Where do submissions fail?" *Confirms if:* high spreadsheet share, frequent data-quality rejections.
6. **A third-party technical manager (DOC holder) running many owners** — tests multi-principal billing/audit pain.
7. **A FuelEU pooling participant** — "How do you reconcile pool-effect allocation with your charterers?" *Confirms if:* pooling settlement is contested.
8. **A Greek maritime-tech investor or accelerator (e.g., VentureFriends, The Dock)** — reality-check on whether the wedge is fundable/defensible vs incumbents.

## Caveats

- **Vendor-marketing vs independent evidence.** The great majority of capability and customer-count claims (OceanScore 2,300–2,500 vessels/100+ customers/~USD 5m ARR; Hecla 1,000+; BetterSea 5,000 "onboarding"; LR VERS 70 companies/€100m) are self-reported and were not independently audited. Treat feature tables as claims.
- **Pricing is opaque by design.** No major platform publishes per-vessel prices; the "small owners are priced out" conclusion is inferred from the quote-based model, managed-service positioning, and explicit spreadsheet acknowledgements — not from a published price list. Validate real willingness-to-pay in Stage 0.
- **No hard Greek survey figure.** I did not locate an independent survey quantifying the spreadsheet share among Greek 3–40-ship owners. The under-penetration claim is a reasoned inference from adoption patterns (large owners named, small owners absent) and industry statements, not a measured statistic.
- **Regulatory uncertainty is high.** The IMO NZF outcome (Dec 2026) is genuinely undecided amid US/oil-state opposition; the EU revision is a *proposal* targeting Q1-2027 adoption and may change in trilogue; UK–EU ETS linkage is stated intent only. Build against what is certain (EU today, UK ETS live, EU "report once" likely) and treat NZF as option value.
- **Arbitration values seen so far are small** (London 4/26: ~€12.7k penalty, 475 EUAs). The disputes market is real but early; the evidence-pack thesis rests on trajectory, not current deal size.
- **Competitor response risk.** The charterer-audit wedge is defensible only until an incumbent with verified-data access (Veson, DNV, LR) decides to ship it. Speed and Piraeus distribution are the moat, not technology.
